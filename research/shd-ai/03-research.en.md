## 8. Strand 5 — AI, educational inequality and opportunity to learn

This strand has particular potential for informing educational policy.

Research on AI often addresses disparities in access to technology, or the digital divide. As AI becomes more widely available, a different form of inequality may emerge: differences in the quality of opportunities for intellectual development available to learners.

### 8.1. Four layers of inequality in the AI era

<div data-viz="inequality"></div>

A hypothesis worth testing is that AI may narrow achievement disparities in the short term while widening gaps in independent competence over time, depending on how learners use AI and the quality of pedagogical support.

For example, when using the same AI tutor, students with strong prior knowledge might request counterexamples, scrutinise arguments and explore alternative solutions. Students with weaker prior knowledge might primarily use the tool to complete tasks. The reverse effect is equally plausible if an AI tutor is appropriately designed to support learners who need additional scaffolding.

### 8.2. Relevance to Vietnam

The PISA 2022 country profile indicates that Vietnam has relatively favourable attainment at minimum proficiency levels, while the proportion of high-performing students remains below the OECD average.

<div data-viz="pisa"></div>

These findings motivate investigations into how AI might support higher-order competence and identify developmental potential among students from different socioeconomic backgrounds. PISA 2022 data do not directly establish the effects of GenAI.

Evidence from Nigeria is also relevant: a six-week GenAI tutoring programme reported a positive effect of 0.23 SD on English-language outcomes. This study indicates the potential for deploying AI in resource-constrained education systems. [Source: World Bank Working Paper](https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-11125).

## 9. Strand 6 — Relational pedagogy, human agency and well-being

An important theme in the interview concerns teachers' roles in listening, encouraging, understanding and creating developmental opportunities for individual learners.

It should not be assumed that AI is incapable of providing any form of emotional support. Instead, research should investigate differences between human and AI support, particularly in their longer-term developmental consequences.

### 9.1. Theoretical foundations to extend

| Field | Theoretical orientation |
|---|---|
| Philosophy of education | Educational purpose; subjectification |
| Motivational psychology | Self-Determination Theory |
| Relational pedagogy | Teacher–student relationships |
| Trust and engagement | School belonging; epistemic trust |
| Occupational well-being | Job Demands–Resources Model |
| Economics of education | Time allocation; opportunity cost |

### 9.2. Research questions

AI may free teachers from administrative work. Does the time saved translate into higher-quality interactions with students, or is it absorbed by other occupational demands?

Does AI-generated feedback produce levels of confidence, persistence and resilience following failure comparable to those associated with feedback from teachers?

When teachers use AI to understand and support students, does it enhance their understanding or increase reliance on incomplete representations of learners?

## 11. An overarching scientific problem with potential for substantial contribution

Comparing the seven strands does not imply that all 35 studies deserve equal priority.

The problem with the greatest potential to connect disciplines is:

### The conversion of AI-augmented performance into human capability

The central question is not how much work AI can perform on behalf of people, but how much developmental value people acquire through its use.

A simple conceptual dynamic can be expressed as:

$$C_{t+1}=C_t+L_t-D_t$$

Here, $C_t$ denotes competence at time $t$, $L_t$ represents competence gained through learning, and $D_t$ represents losses arising from insufficient practice or other mechanisms.

AI-assisted performance can be represented separately:

$$P_t^{AI}=f(C_t,A_t,T_t,J_t)$$

Here, $A_t$ denotes the characteristics of AI assistance, $T_t$ the task characteristics, and $J_t$ the quality of judgment and orchestration.

These expressions are conceptual representations, not validated mathematical models. Their purpose is to distinguish longer-term competence from immediate task performance.
