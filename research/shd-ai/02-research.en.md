## 4. Strand 1 — Learning, cognitive development and human–AI interaction

This is the foundational research strand. When machines can perform an intellectual task, what activities must human learners undertake to genuinely learn from it?

### 4.1. Research gap

Research on cognitive offloading, ICAP, productive failure and recent GenAI experiments suggests that technological assistance can yield divergent learning outcomes.

A candidate research gap concerns the process through which knowledge supplied by AI becomes knowledge that learners can independently reconstruct, transform and apply in unfamiliar situations.

| ID | Proposed research title |

| L02 | When Cognitive Offloading Becomes Cognitive Development |
| L03 | The Effects of AI Assistance Timing on Independent Learning and Transfer |

| L05 | Longitudinal Effects of AI Dependence on Adaptive Expertise |

L01 could begin with mathematics, particularly the understanding of proofs, and subsequently extend to physics, the natural sciences and other disciplinary contexts.

### 4.3. Illustrative experimental design

A randomised 2 × 2 design manipulates the timing of access to an AI-generated solution and the requirement for independent reconstruction.

| Group | Timing of AI solution access | Independent reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | At the outset | Not required |
| G2 | At the outset | Required |
| G3 | After an initial problem-solving attempt | Not required |
| G4 | After an initial problem-solving attempt | Required |

Groups receive equivalent disciplinary content, with learning time and support conditions controlled. A conventional instructional control group could be added if resources permit.

Outcomes should include conceptual understanding, explanation, independent reconstruction, near transfer, far transfer and retention after two to four weeks.

### 4.4. Potential scientific contribution

If specific reconstruction activities enable learners to convert AI-supplied solutions into independently demonstrated competence, the study would move beyond simply establishing beneficial or detrimental effects of AI.

Conversely, if reconstruction provides no benefit beyond time spent practising and prior knowledge, the hypothesised distinctive mechanism must be revised.

Prospective journals: *Cognition*, *Journal of Educational Psychology*, *Learning and Instruction*, *Journal of the Learning Sciences*, *Educational Psychology Review*.

## 5. Strand 2 — Educational measurement and assessment in human–AI systems

This strand is particularly amenable to early implementation and may offer substantial scientific value if it addresses fundamental measurement problems.

### 5.1. Scientific problem

Learning products have conventionally been used as a basis for inferences about the competence of their authors. As AI participates more extensively in producing these artefacts, the relationship may change.

The problem is not merely academic misconduct. It concerns how much information about individual competence is contained in an observed outcome.

<div data-viz="paradox"></div>

### 5.2. Proposed measurement model

A simple measurement model offers a starting point:

$$Y_{ijc}=\alpha_{jc}+\lambda_{jc}\theta_i+\varepsilon_{ijc}$$

| Symbol | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| $Y_{ijc}$ | Observed performance of learner $i$ on task $j$ under assistance condition $c$ |
| $\theta_i$ | Individual competence about which an inference is intended |
| $\alpha_{jc}$ | Baseline performance for the task and condition |
| $\lambda_{jc}$ | Extent to which task performance reflects the target competence |
| $\varepsilon_{ijc}$ | Error and other unmodelled factors |

The testable hypothesis is that AI may, under some conditions, raise mean scores while reducing the extent to which observed performance reflects individual competence.

An empirical model must also accommodate AI-use competence, learner–tool interactions, task characteristics, rater reliability and measurement invariance across conditions.

### 5.3. Proposed studies

| ID | Proposed research title |
|---|---|
| A01 | When High-Quality Student Work Stops Being Evidence of Independent Competence |
| A02 | The Performance–Diagnosticity Paradox in AI-Mediated Assessment |
| A03 | Incremental Validity of Process Evidence in Human–AI Co-Production |
| A04 | False-Competence Classification under Generative AI Assistance |
| A05 | Measuring Independent and AI-Augmented Competence: A Multidimensional Approach |

A03 could test whether adding process traces, decision justifications and transfer tasks improves prediction of subsequent practical competence.

Out-of-sample validation is essential: showing that additional predictors increase $R^2$ within the development sample is insufficient.

Prospective journals: *Educational Assessment*, *Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education*, *Journal of Educational Measurement*, *Applied Measurement in Education*, *Studies in Educational Evaluation*.

## 6. Strand 3 — Teacher professional judgment, agency and expertise

This strand builds directly on PGJ and previous research on teacher education.

The [UNESCO AI competency framework for teachers (2024)](https://doi.org/10.54675/ZJTE2084) specifies 15 competencies across five dimensions: a human-centred mindset, ethics of AI, AI foundations and applications, AI pedagogy, and AI for professional development.

A further inventory of teacher AI competencies is therefore not the primary research priority. A more consequential question is how teachers sustain, develop or lose professional expertise as AI undertakes an increasing share of professional work.

### 6.1. Three phenomena warranting investigation

| Phenomenon | Testable question |
|---|---|
| Professional augmentation | Under what conditions does AI help teachers identify and resolve pedagogical problems more effectively? |
| Professional deskilling | Does repeatedly delegating instructional design and assessment to AI undermine independent professional performance? |
| Adaptive expertise | Can teachers apply disciplinary and pedagogical knowledge to unfamiliar situations for which AI has not previously provided assistance? |

### 6.2. Proposed studies

| ID | Proposed research title |
|---|---|
| T01 | Pedagogical GenAI Judgment: Construct and Discriminant Validation |
| T02 | Beyond AI Literacy: Measuring Teachers’ Situated Professional Judgment |
| T03 | From AI-Assisted Lesson Planning to Independent Teaching Expertise |
| T04 | Does Generative AI Accelerate or Erode Teacher Professional Learning? |
| T05 | Teacher Autonomy and Accountability in Agentic AI Classrooms |

### 6.3. Promising empirical investigation

Follow pre-service teachers over one or two semesters, examining:

- The quality of lesson plans produced with AI assistance.
- The ability to identify students' misconceptions.
- The selection of pedagogical strategies when AI makes inappropriate recommendations.
- Performance in microteaching and authentic classroom instruction.
- Independent competence following the gradual withdrawal of assistance.
- The ability to justify and take responsibility for professional decisions.

The explanatory and predictive value of PGJ should be evaluated against pedagogical content knowledge (PCK), technological pedagogical content knowledge (TPACK), AI competence, evaluative judgement and metacognition.

If PGJ does not explain additional variance or predict professional behaviour better than existing constructs, its theoretical scope should be narrowed rather than maintaining construct independence by assertion.

Prospective journals: *Teaching and Teacher Education*, *Journal of Teacher Education*, *European Journal of Teacher Education*, *Journal of Research on Technology in Education*.

## 7. Strand 4 — Human intelligence, creativity and talent development in the AI era

This strand provides a particularly promising extension beyond PGJ and could develop a distinctive research identity.

### 7.1. The creativity–convergence paradox

[Doshi and Hauser (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290) found that AI could enhance individual creativity while reducing collective creative diversity. A 2026 synthesis was also identified as suggesting that homogenisation depends on the organisation of human–AI interaction; the source of that synthesis requires verification.

This raises a new educational question: might education increasingly reward students for producing higher-quality artefacts while their approaches to thinking, problem selection and problem solving become less diverse?

<div data-viz="convergence"></div>

### 7.2. Identifying talent when AI-assisted achievement is insufficiently informative

[Card and Giuliano (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1605043113), in PNAS, demonstrated that screening practices influence the identification of gifted children from socioeconomically disadvantaged backgrounds.

AI complicates this issue because it may both compensate for resource disadvantages and obscure distinctions between current performance and developmental potential.

A research approach to talent identification could examine:

- The rate of learning when encountering an unfamiliar concept.
- Independent identification of a problem's underlying structure.
- Counterexample generation.
- Flexibility in strategy selection.
- Generalisation and generation of new problems.
- Transfer following the withdrawal of support.

### 7.3. Proposed studies

| ID | Proposed research title |
|---|---|
| C01 | The Creativity–Convergence Paradox in AI-Supported Education |
| C02 | Identifying Human Talent When AI Can Produce Exceptional Performance |
| C03 | Dynamic Assessment of Mathematical Potential in Human–AI Learning |
| C04 | From Mathematical Proof Verification to Generative Mathematical Understanding |
| C05 | Epistemic Originality in the Age of Artificial Intelligence |

C02 and C03 connect cognitive science, gifted education, mathematics and educational measurement.

Unfamiliar problems could replace widely rehearsed examination items, with controls for prior knowledge, Olympiad preparation, access to AI and prior instructional support.

Prospective journals: *Intelligence*, *Gifted Child Quarterly*, *Journal for Research in Mathematics Education*, *Educational Studies in Mathematics*, *Thinking Skills and Creativity*.
