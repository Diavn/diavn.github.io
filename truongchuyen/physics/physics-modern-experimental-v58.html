1. Giản đồ không-thời gian và bất biến

Lý thuyết và suy dẫn

PHY206-A · Special relativity

Dr. Dang Minh Tuan

Review draft; attempt problems before opening solutions.

1. Spacetime diagram and invariants

Theory and derivation

Two events have separation (cΔt,Δx) in S. Lorentz transformation gives \(c\Delta t'=\gamma(c\Delta t-\beta\Delta x),\ \Delta x'=\gamma(\Delta x-\beta c\Delta t)\), β=v/c. Squaring and subtracting cancels cross terms, leaving \(s^2=(c\Delta t)^2-(\Delta x)^2\). Timelike pairs can share a position in some frame; spacelike pairs can be simultaneous in some frame but cannot be connected by a subluminal signal. Distinguish proper from coordinate time.

Worked example

cΔt=5 m, Δx=3 m: s²=16 m². Frame β=3/5 has Δx'=0, cΔt'=4 m; Δτ=4/c.

Independent practice

PHY206-A-V58-1.1. cΔt=3 m, Δx=5 m. Classify interval.

PHY206-A-V58-1.2. Two events have cΔt=2 m, Δx=4 m. Find β for simultaneity.

Application and transfer

Clock synchronization and accelerators; GPS additionally requires general-relativistic corrections.

2. Xuyên hầm và giới hạn gần đúng

Lý thuyết và suy dẫn

PHY206-B · Quantum and atomic physics

Dr. Dang Minh Tuan

Review draft; attempt problems before opening solutions.

1. Quantum wells and probability

Theory and derivation

An infinite well 0<x<L has \(\psi_n(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)\). Zero boundary values and stationary Schrödinger equation give \(E_n=n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/(2mL^2)\). Normalization follows from integral of sin² equal to L/2. Probability in [a,b] is the integral of probability density, not ψ itself. Energy scales as n² and L⁻². Energy eigenstate density is time independent.

Worked example

Electron, L=1 nm: E₁≈0.376 eV, E₂≈1.504 eV; ΔE≈1.128 eV. Use h²/(8mL²).

Independent practice

PHY206-B-V58-1.1. Double L. How does E₁ change?

PHY206-B-V58-1.2. Find probability for n=1 in [0,L/4].

Application and transfer

Semiconductor quantum wells: ideal confinement model; finite wells have different boundary conditions.

2. Tunnelling and approximation limits

Theory and derivation

For constant V₀>E, Schrödinger equation gives combinations of exp(±κx), \(\kappa=\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar\). For a sufficiently thick rectangular barrier, leading transmission dependence is \(T\propto e^{-2\kappa a}\), with prefactor from boundary matching; not exact for arbitrary width. Probability current is conserved for real static potentials.

Worked example

κ=5 nm⁻¹, increase a from 1 to 1.2 nm at fixed energy: approximate transmission ratio exp(−2)=0.1353.

Independent practice

PHY206-B-V58-2.1. For a sufficiently thick rectangular quantum barrier with decay constant κ=2 nm⁻¹, increase barrier width by 0.5 nm while holding particle energy and barrier height fixed. Estimate the ratio of transmission probabilities in the leading exponential approximation.

PHY206-B-V58-2.2. At fixed V₀−E and a, quadruple m. Changes in κ and exponent?

Application and transfer

Scanning tunnelling microscopy: current sensitivity to separation and model limits.

Theory and derivation

A one-dimensional chain has masses m, spacing a and springs k. Newton gives \(m\ddot u_j=k(u_{j+1}+u_{j-1}-2u_j)\). Insert \(u_j=Ue^{i(qja-\omega t)}\) to obtain \(\omega^2=4k/m\sin^2(qa/2)\). Dr. Dang Minh Tuan

1. Carriers and electrical conductivity

Theory and derivation

In a low-field model, semiconductor conductivity is \(\sigma=e(n\mu_n+p\mu_p)\). n,p are electron and hole concentrations, μ positive mobilities. Both conventional currents align with field although electron velocity opposes it. Nondegenerate equilibrium gives np=ni². Fully ionized donors give n≈ND only when ND≫ni and acceptor compensation is negligible. Not for high-field or degenerate material.

Worked example

n=10²¹ m⁻³, μn=0.1 m²/(V s), neglect holes: σ=16 S/m.

Independent practice

PHY207-B-V58-1.1. Double n at fixed μn, neglect holes. Resistivity change?

PHY207-B-V58-1.2. ni=10¹⁶ m⁻³, n=10²⁰ m⁻³. Infer p and n/p.

Application and transfer

Material characterization via conductivity and Hall effect; one measurement alone cannot separate n and μ.

2. Photodetection and energy limits

Theory and derivation

Photon energy E=hc/λ. In a band-gap absorption model, threshold λg=hc/Eg; photons above threshold may create carrier pairs, but actual absorption depends on material and transitions. For external quantum efficiency η (collected pairs per incident photon), \(I=e\eta P/(hf)\) and responsivity \(R=e\eta\lambda/(hc)\). Quantum efficiency is not power-conversion efficiency.

Worked example

Eg=1.12 eV: λg≈1107 nm. λ=800 nm, η=0.75: R≈0.484 A/W.

Independent practice

PHY207-B-V58-2.2. Detector R=0.5 A/W at 1000 nm, P=20 μW. Find I and η.

Application and transfer

Theory and derivation

At constant λ, \(N(t)=N_0e^{-\lambda t}\), T₁/₂=ln2/λ. Counts over a time window are random; Poisson variance equals expectation. 1. Uncertainty propagation with correlation

Theory and derivation

For y=f(x,z), first-order Taylor gives δy≈fxδx+fzδz. Variance yields \(u_y^2=f_x^2u_x^2+f_z^2u_z^2+2f_xf_z\operatorname{cov}(x,z)\). Independence removes the cross term.
